# Stage 1 interview guide

Purpose: validate the problem and find the real user, the moment that matters,
and the data that exists. Do not pitch the product in the first half.

Target: 5-8 interviews across textile, food and light engineering. At minimum,
two supervisors or training officers and one senior operator or technician.

## Consent and recording

- Explain the purpose and that notes are used for a student project.
- Ask before recording; offer to keep company and people anonymous.
- Do not ask for real SOPs, HR records or personal data in the first meeting.

## Warm-up (5 min)

1. What does your role cover on a normal shift?
2. How many people do you supervise or train, and what languages do they read?

## The last breakdown (15 min)

3. Tell me about the last machine breakdown. What happened, who fixed it, how long
   did it take, and what did it cost?
4. Where did the person who fixed it learn that method?
5. What happens when that person is on leave or leaves the company?

## Training and language (15 min)

6. Walk me through how a new operator is trained today, step by step.
7. Which instructions exist in writing, and where do they live (paper, Excel,
   ERP, wall)?
8. Which parts are only explained verbally? In which language?
9. What goes wrong when an operator does not understand an instruction?

## Data and tools (10 min)

10. What records do you keep about faults, quality and training, and in what form?
11. Which tools does the team already use daily (phone, WhatsApp, Excel, ERP)?
12. What would you not be willing to share, and why?

## Value (5 min)

13. If the same question stopped coming back, what would that be worth in hours
    or rupees per week?
14. What would make you trust a tool like this enough to let an operator use it?

## Close

15. Who else should we speak to?
16. Would you be open to a 30-minute follow-up to review a prototype?

## What to listen for

- **The repeated question**: the same explanation given more than once a week.
- **The absent expert**: a method that exists only in one person's head.
- **The language gap**: an instruction that exists but is not readable.
- **The paper trail**: where the "real" record lives.
- **The trust condition**: what would have to be true before they let an operator
  act on an AI answer.
